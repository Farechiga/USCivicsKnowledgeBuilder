## Questions

### Q001 — What is the form of government of the United States?

[q001_question]  What is the form of government of the United States?

[q001_lead_in]  You could describe it any of these ways (you only need one):

[q001_answer_1]  Republic

[q001_answer_2]  Constitution-based federal republic

[q001_answer_3]  Representative democracy

[q001_disambiguation]  We most often hear it called a “representative democracy” — the same idea as a “republic”: we elect people to make the laws for us. That's different from a “direct democracy,” where people vote on every law themselves.

[q001_why]  In a republic, the power belongs to the people — not a king. We lend it to leaders we elect, and take it back at the next election. In 1787, when kings ruled almost everywhere, trusting regular people to run things was a bold bet.

[q001_fun]  After the Convention, someone asked Benjamin Franklin: did we make a republic or a monarchy? “A republic,” he said, “if you can keep it.” His point: this government was brand-new, and keeping it would take work from every generation.


### Q002 — What is the supreme law of the land?

[q002_question]  What is the supreme law of the land?

[q002_answer_1]  (U.S.) Constitution

[q002_disambiguation]  Don't mix these up: the “supreme law of the land” is the Constitution itself — not the “Supreme Court.” One is the rulebook; the other is the referee.

[q002_why]  Even the President has to obey the Constitution. That's the big idea: no one in America is above the law — not even the most powerful person. The law itself is the boss.

[q002_fun]  Long ago, Maryland tried to tax a national bank to death. The Supreme Court said no — one state can't destroy what the whole country builds. That's how we learned: when state and national law collide, national law wins.


### Q003 — Name one thing the U.S. Constitution does.

[q003_question]  Name one thing the U.S. Constitution does.

[q003_lead_in]  You only need to name one — the Constitution does all of these:

[q003_answer_1]  Forms the government

[q003_answer_2]  Defines powers of government

[q003_answer_3]  Defines the parts of government

[q003_answer_4]  Protects the rights of the people

[q003_why]  In 1787, most countries ran on a king's word or unwritten custom. The Constitution did something new — it wrote the rules down. It builds the government (a Congress to make laws, a President to enforce them, courts to judge) and limits it too, by protecting people's rights.

[q003_fun]  Of every written national constitution on Earth, the U.S. Constitution is both the oldest still in use and one of the shortest — only about 4,500 words. The whole rulebook for the government is shorter than many a school essay.


### Q004 — The U.S. Constitution starts with the words “We the People.” What does “We the People” mean?

[q004_question]  The U.S. Constitution starts with the words “We the People.” What does “We the People” mean?

[q004_lead_in]  You only need one — these all point at the same big idea:

[q004_answer_1]  Self-government

[q004_answer_2]  Popular sovereignty

[q004_answer_3]  Consent of the governed

[q004_answer_4]  People should govern themselves

[q004_answer_5]  (Example of) social contract

[q004_disambiguation]  All of these mean the same thing in different words: the people are in charge of the government, not the other way around.

[q004_why]  These words flipped history. For ages, kings held the power and the people obeyed. “We the People” says the opposite: the government's power comes from regular people — and belongs to them.

[q004_fun]  An early draft of the Constitution started by naming all thirteen states, one by one. A delegate changed it to just three words: “We the People.” That small change meant the country belonged to its people — not to thirteen separate states.


### Q005 — How are changes made to the U.S. Constitution?

[q005_question]  How are changes made to the U.S. Constitution?

[q005_lead_in]  Either answer works:

[q005_answer_1]  Amendments

[q005_answer_2]  The amendment process

[q005_why]  The Founders knew they wouldn't get everything right. So they built in a way to fix the Constitution — by adding amendments — but made it hard, so it changes only when almost the whole country agrees.

[q005_fun]  People have tried to change the Constitution more than 11,000 times. Only 27 of those changes ever made it in. It's that hard on purpose — so the rules change only when nearly everyone agrees.


### Q006 — What does the Bill of Rights protect?

[q006_question]  What does the Bill of Rights protect?

[q006_lead_in]  Either answer works:

[q006_answer_1]  (The basic) rights of Americans

[q006_answer_2]  (The basic) rights of people living in the United States

[q006_disambiguation]  Notice: it protects the rights of everyone living in the United States — not only citizens.

[q006_why]  The Bill of Rights flips the usual question. Instead of listing what people may do, it lists what the government may NOT do to them — like silence your speech or your religion. These are freedoms no leader can take away.

[q006_fun]  Our famous First Amendment — free speech and religion — was almost the Third! Twelve changes were proposed; the first two didn't pass back then, so number three jumped to the top. Even our rights were chosen one by one.

[q006_mnemonic]  Count the Bill of Rights on your hands: ten amendments, ten fingers.

## Vocabulary (record ONCE — shared across every question)

### Republic

[term_republic_origin]  From the Latin “res publica” — “the public affair.” A republic is a government that belongs to the public, not to a king.

[term_republic_def]  A country where people pick leaders to make decisions for them — instead of a king deciding everything.

[term_republic_word]  Republic   _(say slowly and clearly — the app repeats it)_


### Representative democracy

[term_representative_democracy_def]  A government where we vote for people to represent us and make the laws on our behalf.

[term_representative_democracy_word]  Representative democracy   _(say slowly and clearly — the app repeats it)_


### Popular sovereignty

[term_popular_sovereignty_origin]  A “sovereign” is a supreme ruler, like a king. Sovereignty is the power to rule — so “popular sovereignty” hands that power to the people.

[term_popular_sovereignty_def]  Sovereignty means the power to rule. Popular sovereignty means that power belongs to the people.

[term_popular_sovereignty_word]  Popular sovereignty   _(say slowly and clearly — the app repeats it)_


### Consent of the governed

[term_consent_of_the_governed_def]  The government only has power because the people agree to give it. “Consent” means to say yes.

[term_consent_of_the_governed_word]  Consent of the governed   _(say slowly and clearly — the app repeats it)_


### Social contract

[term_social_contract_origin]  Think of a contract — an agreement. The “social contract” is the unwritten agreement between people and their government.

[term_social_contract_def]  An unwritten deal: people agree to follow a government's laws, and in return the government protects them.

[term_social_contract_word]  Social contract   _(say slowly and clearly — the app repeats it)_


### Self-government

[term_self_government_def]  When people rule themselves, instead of being ruled by a king or anyone else.

[term_self_government_word]  Self-government   _(say slowly and clearly — the app repeats it)_


### Amendment

[term_amendment_origin]  Amend means to change — like mending a hole in a dress. (“Mend” even comes from “amend.”)

[term_amendment_def]  An amendment is an official change or addition to the Constitution.

[term_amendment_word]  Amendment   _(say slowly and clearly — the app repeats it)_


### Bill of Rights

[term_bill_of_rights_def]  The first ten amendments to the Constitution. It lists freedoms the government can't take away.

[term_bill_of_rights_word]  Bill of Rights   _(say slowly and clearly — the app repeats it)_


### Constitution

[term_constitution_def]  The rulebook for the whole country. It sets up the government and protects people's rights.

[term_constitution_word]  Constitution   _(say slowly and clearly — the app repeats it)_
